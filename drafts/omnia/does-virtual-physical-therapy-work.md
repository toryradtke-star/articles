Yes, for many people virtual physical therapy can work well, especially when the care is built around movement assessment, exercise, and education rather than hands-on treatment. A telehealth PT visit is a live, one-on-one video session where a Doctor of Physical Therapy talks through your symptoms, watches you move, and coaches you through a plan you can do at home.

Omnia Wellness & Recovery offers virtual visits to people across Minnesota and Wisconsin, and serves Superior and Duluth in person from its clinic at 1308 Tower Ave, Superior, WI 54880.

## Does virtual physical therapy actually work?

Virtual physical therapy can be a helpful option for many people because much of what helps in physical therapy, including guided exercise, movement retraining, and education, does not require being in the same room.

From a physical therapist's perspective, much of the care for many common muscle and joint concerns comes down to the same core pieces whether a visit happens in the clinic or on video: a careful assessment, a well-matched exercise plan, and steady practice between sessions. Results still vary from person to person, and no format guarantees a particular outcome.

What tends to matter most is the quality of the assessment, how well the program fits your goals, and whether you do the work between sessions. A one-on-one video visit with a Doctor of Physical Therapy keeps the focus on you: your history, your movement, and your daily life.

Seeing you at home can also be an advantage. A DPT can watch how you get up from your actual couch, climb your actual stairs, or use the equipment you really have, which can make a program easier to stick with.

## What happens during a telehealth PT visit?

A telehealth PT visit is a live video appointment that generally follows the same structure as an in-person evaluation, minus the hands-on treatment. A typical session moves through a few steps:

1. **Your story.** You talk through what's going on: when it started, what makes it better or worse, what you've already tried, and what you want to get back to doing.
2. **Movement assessment.** Your DPT watches you move on camera, such as squatting, bending, reaching, walking, or balancing, often from more than one angle.
3. **Guided self-tests.** You're walked through simple ways to check strength, range of motion, and which movements bring on your symptoms.
4. **Exercise coaching.** You learn exercises in real time, with feedback on form, pacing, and how hard to push.
5. **A plan.** You leave with a home program, a sense of how to progress it, what to watch for, and when to check in next.

Because the visit is one-on-one, there's room to ask questions and adjust the plan to your schedule and space. ## What problems are a good fit for virtual physical therapy?

Virtual physical therapy may be a good fit when the main tools are exercise, movement coaching, and education. Common examples include:

- Ongoing back, neck, or joint stiffness where building strength and mobility is the main goal
- Starting or progressing a strength or weightlifting program with proper technique
- Flexibility and mobility training
- Injury prevention for runners, lifters, and recreational athletes
- Continuing a home program after starting care in person
- People in greater Minnesota and Wisconsin, or with busy schedules, where driving to a clinic is a barrier

These are general examples, not a diagnosis. A DPT can help you decide whether video care makes sense for your situation, and you can browse the full list of [physical therapy services at Omnia Wellness & Recovery](/services) to see what's offered.

## When is in-person physical therapy the better choice?

In-person physical therapy is usually the better choice when your care calls for hands-on treatment, imaging, or a closer physical exam. Virtual visits also have limits for assessment: a physical therapist can't feel tissue, test joints by hand, or see inside an injury through a camera. Some things simply can't happen through a screen:

- **Dry needling with electrical stimulation** and **cupping**, which are done in the clinic
- **Manual therapy and manipulation therapy**, including joint mobilization and soft-tissue work
- **Diagnostic ultrasound**, which Omnia Wellness & Recovery offers as real-time, in-clinic imaging of muscles, tendons, and joints
- Situations where a hands-on exam would help clarify what's going on
- Times when moving safely on your own at home is difficult, or when technology gets in the way

A hybrid approach is also an option: an in-person visit at the Superior clinic for hands-on work or imaging, then virtual sessions to keep your home program moving. If you're curious about the hands-on side of care, you can [meet Emily Holter, DPT, DN](/news/meet-emily), an orthopedic physical therapist with 15 years of experience whose favorite work includes dry needling, myofascial release, and return-to-sport assessments.

If you have sudden severe pain, spreading numbness or weakness, loss of bladder or bowel control, chest pain, or symptoms after a significant fall or accident, seek urgent medical care rather than booking a physical therapy visit.

## How do I prepare for a virtual PT visit?

Preparing for a virtual PT visit mostly comes down to having a steady video connection, room to move, and clothing that lets your physical therapist see how you move. A few minutes of setup makes the session much more useful:

- **A device with a camera** you can prop up so it shows your whole body, not just your face. A phone on a shelf or a laptop on a chair works.
- **Clear floor space** a few steps wide, plus a sturdy chair and a nearby wall or counter.
- **Comfortable, loose-fitting clothing** you can move in. Athletic wear is ideal, and shorts help for knee or hip concerns.
- **Whatever equipment you already have**, like resistance bands, dumbbells, or a foam roller. You usually don't need to buy anything before your first visit.
- **A few notes**: when symptoms started, what aggravates them, any imaging reports, and your goals.
- **Good lighting and a quiet spot**, so your PT can see and hear you clearly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is virtual physical therapy as effective as in-person?
For many people, virtual PT built around exercise, movement coaching, and education can be a helpful option, though results vary and hands-on treatments such as dry needling, cupping, and manual therapy still require an in-person visit.

### Do I need special equipment for a telehealth PT visit?
No. You need a device with a camera, some clear floor space, and comfortable clothing. Your PT can build your program around whatever equipment you already have.
