You usually don't need a referral to start physical therapy for a common muscle or joint problem in Wisconsin or Minnesota, but each state sets limits. Wisconsin law starts with a referral requirement and then lists exceptions that cover many musculoskeletal injuries, though acute fractures and soft tissue avulsions are excluded. Minnesota lets a physical therapist treat you without a referral for an initial period of up to 90 days.

Who pays for your care matters too. An insurance plan, Medicare, or a workers' compensation claim can have its own referral rules on top of state law. This guide covers both states and the questions to ask before you book.

## Can you see a physical therapist without a referral in Wisconsin?

In Wisconsin, you can usually see a physical therapist without a referral for a muscle or joint injury, because the state's referral requirement has broad exceptions.

[Wisconsin Statute 448.56](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/statutes/448.56) starts by requiring a written referral from a physician or certain other licensed practitioners, such as a physician assistant, chiropractor, dentist, or podiatrist. It then lists situations where no referral is needed, including:

- Services related to athletic activities, conditioning, or injury prevention
- Treatment of a previously diagnosed condition, after the therapist informs the practitioner who made the diagnosis
- Certain school-based services

The Wisconsin Physical Therapy Examining Board's rule, [Wisconsin Admin. Code PT 6.01](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/administrativecode/PT%206.01), adds to that list. It covers conditioning, injury prevention, and treatment of musculoskeletal injuries related to work, home, leisure, recreational, and educational settings, but acute fractures and soft tissue avulsions are excluded.

In practice, a sore knee from getting back into running or a stiff back after a weekend of yard work can generally be evaluated and treated without a referral. A fresh fracture, or an injury where a tendon or ligament has pulled away from the bone, calls for a physician's involvement.

Physical therapists are also expected to refer you to an appropriate health care provider if your symptoms or findings suggest a condition beyond the scope of physical therapy.

## Can you see a physical therapist without a referral in Minnesota?

In Minnesota, a physical therapist can evaluate and treat you without a referral for an initial period of up to 90 days.

Under [Minnesota Statute 148.76](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/148.76), care that continues beyond that period generally needs an order or referral from a physician or another licensed provider listed in the statute. A few details matter:

- **The 90-day window:** The statute sets out how the 90 days are counted, so ask your therapist at your first visit how your plan of care fits within it.
- **Previously diagnosed conditions:** The statute includes an exception related to conditions that were previously diagnosed, and your therapist can check whether it applies to you.
- **Prevention and wellness:** Services focused on prevention, wellness, education, or exercise may be treated differently from treatment of a condition.

For many people in Duluth and across Minnesota, a plan of care for a common muscle or joint problem may fit within 90 days. If your problem is longer-term, knowing about the limit at the start helps your therapist plan around it.

## When might you still need a referral?

Even when state law doesn't require a referral, insurance plans and other payers can set their own referral rules, and those apply on top of the law.

- **Insurance plans:** Some plans, especially HMO-style plans, may require a referral or prior authorization. Call the number on your card and ask, "Do I need a referral for outpatient physical therapy?"
- **Medicare:** Medicare has its own requirements for physician involvement in a therapy plan of care, so ask about them before you start.
- **Work injuries, auto claims, and teams:** Workers' compensation, auto claims, employers, and athletic programs may require a physician's involvement or clearance.

## Is it safe to go straight to a physical therapist without seeing a doctor first?

For many common muscle and joint problems, starting with a physical therapist can be a reasonable first step, because Doctors of Physical Therapy are trained to screen for signs that something needs a physician's attention.

During a first visit, a physical therapist typically asks about your history, checks how you move, and looks for warning signs that don't fit a typical muscle or joint pattern. If something doesn't add up, a good therapist will tell you plainly and refer you to a physician.

Some symptoms call for prompt medical care rather than waiting for a therapy appointment, such as:

- Pain after a significant fall or accident
- New numbness in the groin or saddle area, or changes in bladder or bowel control
- Unexplained weight loss, fever, or night pain that doesn't change with position
- Chest pain, shortness of breath, or sudden severe headache

No referral is needed for the ultrasound imaging itself, though the state rules above still apply to your therapy care. It doesn't involve radiation, and it doesn't replace a physician's imaging when that's needed.

## Do you need a referral for a virtual physical therapy visit?

For a virtual physical therapy visit, the referral rules that generally apply are those of the state where you are located during the visit, not the state where you live.

That means someone joining from Duluth follows Minnesota's rules, including the 90-day limit, and someone joining from Superior follows Wisconsin's rules. If you live on one side of the bridge and work on the other, tell your therapist where you'll be joining from.

Omnia Wellness & Recovery offers virtual visits to people across Minnesota and Wisconsin. Insurance referral rules may cover virtual visits too, so check those separately. To see how a video session works, read [what a telehealth PT visit looks like](/news/does-virtual-physical-therapy-work).

Usually not for common muscle and joint injuries. Wisconsin's referral requirement has broad exceptions, but acute fractures and soft tissue avulsions are excluded. Your insurance or another payer may still have its own referral rules.

### How long can I see a physical therapist without a referral in Minnesota?
